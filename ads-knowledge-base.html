Section 01

The 8 Agency Rules (From the Strategy Call)

These came directly from an agency spending $40k+/month on Meta. Every single one of these applies to every client campaign we run.

1

The creative is everything. The creative does the targeting.

Broad targeting, max audience, age 21-50, zero interests. Meta finds the right person. The creative is what qualifies them. If the wrong people are clicking, the problem is the creative, not the audience settings. Never narrow targeting to compensate for weak creative.

2

Every ad opens with a hyper-specific niche call-out.

The very first words must name exactly who this is for. Not "small business owners." Not "service businesses." The specific niche. "If you run an HVAC company." "If you own a home in Cary and Apex." "If you run an AI automation agency." The wrong person should scroll past instantly. The right person should stop immediately.

3

One niche per campaign. Multiple ads, one niche.

On lower budgets you do not split across niches. Each niche needs its own funnel, its own landing page, its own creative. Pick the best niche for that client and go all in. Two similar niches is okay (like HVAC and plumbing) but they are nearly the same person. Adding a completely different niche requires a separate campaign and more spend.

4

CBO, one ad set, broad targeting.

CBO = Campaign Budget Optimization. One campaign, one ad set, all your creatives inside it. Broad targeting means max audience, age 21-50, no interest targeting. Let Meta allocate the budget. When one ad eats most of the spend, that is the system working, not a bug. Meta is a better tester than any human.

5

One primary text. One headline. On every ad.

Primary text has minimal impact on performance. Do not test multiple primary texts or headlines. Write one strong version and use it on every creative in the campaign. The only variable you test is the creative itself. Testing copy variables wastes budget and muddies the data.

6

Never pause the account.

Every time an account goes dark for any reason (payment failure, manual pause, anything) Meta's learning phase resets. It takes 7 to 14 days of uninterrupted spend to re-optimize. A 4-day pause can cost you 2 weeks of performance. Keep the card up to date. Do not pause campaigns to "test" anything. Never pause a winning ad.

7

UGC video first, statics second.

Video always gets better quality leads than static for B2C and local services. The agency runs mostly UGC with one or two statics alongside. The best static they ever ran was a screenshot from the Notes app that took 3 minutes to make. Do not over-design static ads. The lo-fi look stops the scroll better than polished graphics for most niches.

8

Find a winner and repeat it. Do not chase novelty.

The agency has been running the same ad since November 2024 and it is still their best performer. Once you find a winning hook and creative format, make more of the same. 70% of effort goes to repeating proven winners, 20% to variations on winners, 10% to brand new experiments. Most people never find a winner because they keep changing things before anything can optimize.

Section 03

Hormozi: Hooks ($100M Hooks Playbook)

"After you've written your headline, you've spent eighty cents of your advertising dollar." -- David Ogilvy. If you have ten hours to advertise, spend eight of them on the hook.

A hook has two parts: a call-out (this is for me) and a promise of value (if I keep watching I get something). The hook sells the person on paying attention. Nothing else matters if the hook fails.

Distribution of Hormozi's 121 best-performing hooks:

Hook Type% of Winners

Commands21.5%
Questions9.9%
Exclamations9.1%

Stories3%
Conditionals2.5%
Key insight

Almost half of all winning hooks are simple statements. Not questions. Not stories. A declarative sentence that makes the right person think "that's me" or "wait, what?" Write statements first. Questions second. Everything else is secondary.

Labels (Call-Outs)

Name your avatar directly. Works like hearing your own name in a noisy room.

"Local business owners, I have a gift for you."
"If you run an HVAC company..."
"Homeowners in Cary and Apex..."
Statements

The #1 performing type. Make a bold, specific claim. Let it land.

"Your driveway is making your house look older than it is."
"The smartest thing you can do today..."
"How to get ahead of 99% of people."
Questions

Two types: yes questions (lead them to agree) and open questions (create curiosity).

"When's the last time you actually looked at your driveway?"
"Would you pay $1,000 to have the business of your dreams in 30 days?"
Conditionals

"If you [condition], then [result/reward]." Self-selects the audience and promises value in one sentence.

"If you own a home in Cary and Apex, here's exactly what we'll do for you."
"If you're working all the time and your business isn't growing, you're working on the wrong stuff."
Commands

Direct instruction. Works well when paired with a strong benefit.

"Read this if you're tired of being broke."
"Watch this if you want to fill your empty beds."
Hormozi's Best Ad Hooks (Actual)

From his own highest-performing paid ads:

"Real quick question... Can I have your email?"
"Local business owners, I have a gift for you."
"Business owners: Do you ever wonder if you're working on the wrong stuff?"
"Real quick. The reason for this ad is because..."
The 70/20/10 Hook Rule

70% of hooks should be proven winners you already know work. Reuse and remix your best-performing hooks. 20% should be adjacent to winners -- the same formula applied to a new angle. 10% should be brand new experiments. Most businesses do 100% new every time and wonder why nothing sticks.

Hormozi: GOATed Ads System

The GOATed Ads framework is how you scale creative without burning out. You stop thinking of ads as one piece and start assembling them in parts.

Time allocation for recording sessions
  • 80% of prep time on hooks. If they don't make it through the hook, nothing else matters.
  • 20% on the meat. The body gets seen by fewer people so it burns out slower.
  • Almost no time on CTAs. One or two that work, keep using them.
5 types of "meat" (the body)
  1. Demonstration - before/after, unboxing, product in use
  2. Testimonial - UGC, raw iPhone, walk-and-talk rant
  3. Education - explainer, how-to, whiteboard
  4. Story - storytelling, lifestyle, problem-solution
  5. Faceless - screenshots, text only, slideshows
The CTA formula (show AND tell)

A good CTA tells: what to do, how to do it, when to do it, what they get, and optionally what happens next. Most ads only do one of these. Do all five. Then physically demonstrate clicking the button or filling out the form if possible.

"Tap the link below [what + how], right now [when], to get your free quote in 60 seconds [what they get], and we'll call you back same day [what happens next]."

Section 05

Awareness Levels (Eugene Schwartz via Hormozi)

The audience's awareness level determines what type of hook and body you use. Most cold Meta traffic sits at level 4 (problem-aware). Ads start profitably at the bottom and become less profitable as you go colder. Better creative lets you convert colder audiences profitably.

LevelAwareness StateHook StyleBody StyleExample Hook
1. Most AwareKnows you and just needs the dealOffer-drivenJust make the offer"50% off this month only."
2. Product-AwareKnows what you sell, unsure about youProof-drivenShow results and credibility"Why 500 homeowners chose Blue Wave last year."
3. Solution-AwareKnows solutions exist, doesn't know yoursPromise-drivenIntroduce your mechanism as the best path"The fastest way to get your home pressure washed right."
4. Problem-AwareKnows they have a problem, no solution yetPain-drivenAgitate the problem, then present solution"Your driveway is making your house look older than it is."
5. UnawareDoesn't know they have a problemCuriosity-drivenCreate awareness of the hidden problem"The hidden thing that's draining your home value every month."
Where Meta cold traffic usually sits

For local home services (pressure washing, HVAC, etc.) most cold Facebook traffic is at Level 4 (Problem-Aware). They know their driveway is dirty. They know they need it done. They just haven't acted. Your hook should name the problem or the result, not the product. For B2B services like AI receptionist or care home marketing, the audience is often at Level 3 -- they know they're losing leads or missing calls, they just don't know your specific solution.

Section 06

UGC Script Structure

A UGC script is not three sentences. It is 60 to 90 seconds of structured talking. The person filming does not need to be the founder. The tone is casual and direct, like they are telling a neighbor, not performing for a camera. Every script follows the same skeleton.

Master UGC Script Template
1. Niche Call-Out (first 3 seconds -- this is the hook)

Name the exact audience. Be hyper-specific. The wrong person should scroll past. Right person stops. Include location for local services. Include revenue/size qualifier for B2B.

B2C example: "If you own a home in Cary, Apex, Raleigh, or Pittsboro..."
B2B example: "If you run an HVAC company and you're missing calls while your guys are on jobs..."

2. Result + Offer (immediately after the call-out)

Tell them the exact thing you will do for them and the offer. Do not make them wait for this. It comes right after the call-out, not at the end.

"...here's exactly what we'll do. We will come out one time, wash your house and driveway, and have both looking brand new by the end of the day. And right now if you book both together you save $150."

3. How It Works (the mechanism, 2-3 sentences max)

Explain the process simply. What happens, in what order, how long it takes. Answers the "but how does it actually work?" in their head.

4. Proof / Credibility (a real number or result)

A specific number. A client result. A before/after shown on camera. Something that makes the claim believable. "We've done this for X homes" or "here's what last week looked like" with visual.

5. Guarantee (risk removal)

Remove the last objection. Make it specific. "If you're not happy, we come back and redo it free" or "10 jobs in 30 days or you don't pay."

6. CTA (one action, spelled out)

One thing to do. Tap below, link in bio, comment a word. Tell them what they get when they do it and how fast. "Tap the link below, free quote in 60 seconds, I'll get back to you same day."

Filming tips
  • Phone camera is fine, no studio needed
  • Film on location at a job site
  • Casual tone, like telling a neighbor
  • Hold up phone to show before/after during proof section
  • No need to memorize word for word, know the beats
  • Film multiple takes of the hook separately
Common mistakes
  • Leading with the company name instead of a call-out
  • Burying the offer at the end
  • No proof or credibility section
  • Too short (under 45 seconds = not enough to sell)
  • Sounding like an ad instead of a person talking
  • Founder-focused language ("I built this company...") instead of customer-focused
Section 07

The Offer Formula (Hormozi Value Equation)

Sell the result, not the tool. Sell the outcome, not the service. Nobody wants an AI receptionist. They want booked jobs. Nobody wants a pressure wash. They want a home that looks new.
Hormozi's Value Equation

Value = (Dream Outcome x Perceived Likelihood of Success) / (Time Delay x Effort and Sacrifice)

A great offer maximizes the dream outcome and how believable it is, while minimizing how long it takes and how hard it is for the customer. Hit all four or your offer is weak.

Offer ElementWeak VersionStrong Version
Dream Outcome"An AI receptionist for your business""10 extra booked HVAC jobs this month"
Likelihood"Results may vary""10 jobs in 30 days or you don't pay until it happens"
Time Delay"See results over time""Live in 48 hours. Results in 30 days."
Effort"Set it up yourself""Done for you. You just show up to the jobs."

Rules for building the offer:

  • Include a specific number. "More leads" is not an offer. "20-30 qualified appointments" is an offer.
  • Include a time frame. "In 30 days" is concrete. "Over time" is not.
  • Include a guarantee that removes risk. "Or you don't pay" is the gold standard.
  • For local services where pricing varies, use a discount bundle instead of a fixed price. "$150 off when you book both" is concrete even when the total varies.
  • Name the mechanism. "Our Resident Acquisition System" sounds more proprietary than "our service."
Offer examples by client

Scalevertise (care homes): "20-30 qualified resident appointments in 30 days or your management fee is waived. Done for you. One home per area."

Subutai AI (HVAC): "We install an AI receptionist that books you 10 extra jobs from missed calls in your first 30 days, or you don't pay until it does. First 50 calls free."

Blue Wave Pro Wash: "We come out one time, wash your house and driveway, and have both looking brand new same day. Book the bundle and save $150. Limited spots per month."

Section 08

Static Ad Formats

Run statics alongside UGC. Never instead of. Two formats consistently outperform everything else for local and B2B services.

Format 1: Notes App Screenshot (Top Performer)
What it is

A screenshot of a plain text note from your phone. Black text on white. One or two emojis max. No design. No branding. No graphic.

Why it works

Looks organic. Stops the scroll because it doesn't look like an ad. The agency said their single best-performing ad of all time is this format and took 3 minutes to make.

Formula

EMOJI + AUDIENCE CALL-OUT IN CAPS
One sentence: what you'll do for them + the offer.
One sentence: urgency (limited spots, this month only).
CTA with emoji.

Format 2: Before/After Split Image
What it is

Full-width image split vertically. Dirty/bad state on the left. Clean/good state on the right. Bold text overlay.

Why it works

The transformation is the entire ad. For any visual service (pressure washing, cleaning, renovation, landscaping) the before/after does more selling than any copy.

Rules

Use the most dramatic before/after you have. The bigger the visual gap, the better it performs. Bold text on the image: "SAME DRIVEWAY. 2 HOURS." Caption below explains the offer.

Notes app example (Blue Wave Pro Wash)

🏠 CARY, APEX, RALEIGH and PITTSBORO HOMEOWNERS

Book your house wash + driveway as a package this month and save $150.

One visit. Done the same day. Limited spots available.

👇 Get your free quote in 60 seconds.

Section 09

Lead Follow-Up System

The ads get the lead. The follow-up speed decides whether that lead becomes a job. This is the most ignored part of the funnel and the most expensive mistake a client can make.

21x
More likely to close if contacted within 5 minutes vs. 30 minutes
80%
Drop in conversion rate when follow-up happens after 30 minutes
60 sec
How long a motivated lead stays hot before scrolling to the next ad

What actually happens to a slow lead: They fill out the form. They keep scrolling Facebook. A competitor's ad shows up. That company texts them in 30 seconds. By the time your client calls an hour later, the homeowner does not even remember filling out the form and has already booked with someone else. The lead was not bad. It went cold.

The fix: instant automated SMS

The second someone submits the lead form, an automated text fires. This is set up through Zapier connecting Facebook Lead Ads to GoHighLevel or any SMS tool. Takes about 20 minutes to configure. Once live, every lead feels taken care of immediately even when the owner is on a job.

"Hey [First Name]! This is Josh from Blue Wave Pro Wash. Just saw your request come through and I'm pulling together your quote now. I'll give you a call shortly. Feel free to reply here with any questions."

Rule for every client

Instant automated follow-up is non-negotiable before scaling spend. Running ads without it is like filling a bucket with a hole in it. The ads can be perfect and the client will still say the leads are bad quality. They are not bad. They are cold. Fix the follow-up first.

Quick Reference

Checklist Before Launching Any Campaign

Ads checklist
  • Niche call-out is the very first thing said or seen
  • Result + offer come immediately after the call-out
  • Mechanism is explained in 2-3 sentences max
  • Proof or credibility number is included
  • Guarantee is specific and bold
  • CTA tells them exactly what to do
  • No em dashes anywhere in the copy
  • No founder-centered language unless it adds credibility
  • UGC video is at least 45 seconds
  • Static notes-app ad created alongside video
Campaign setup checklist
  • CBO campaign, one ad set
  • Broad targeting, age 21-50, no interests
  • One primary text across all ads
  • One headline across all ads
  • Testing creatives only, not copy
  • One niche targeted per campaign
  • Payment method confirmed before launch
  • Instant SMS follow-up live before launch
  • Let campaign run minimum 7 days before judging
  • Do not pause unless absolutely necessary
