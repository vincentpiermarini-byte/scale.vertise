## PART 3: THE 8 AGENCY RULES (From strategy call with agency spending $40k+/month)

These are non-negotiable for every client campaign.

**Rule 1: The creative is everything. The creative does the targeting.**
Broad targeting, max audience, age 21–50, zero interests. Meta finds the right person. The creative is what qualifies them. If the wrong people are clicking, the problem is the creative, not the audience. Never narrow targeting to compensate for weak creative.

**Rule 2: Every ad opens with a hyper-specific niche call-out.**
The very first words must name exactly who this is for. "If you run an HVAC company." "If you own a home in Cary and Apex." "If you're a care home owner." The wrong person should scroll past instantly. The right person stops. Generic call-outs like "small business owners" do not work at this level.

**Rule 3: One niche per campaign.**
On lower budgets, do not split across niches. Each niche needs its own funnel, its own landing page, its own creative. Pick the best niche and go all in. Two similar niches (HVAC and plumbing) can share a campaign because they're nearly the same person. A completely different niche requires a separate campaign.

**Rule 4: CBO, one ad set, broad targeting.**
CBO = Campaign Budget Optimization. One campaign, one ad set, all creatives inside it. Broad targeting = max audience, age 21–50, no interest targeting. Let Meta allocate the budget. When one ad eats most of the spend, that is the system working. Meta is a better tester than any human.

**Rule 5: One primary text. One headline. On every ad.**
Primary text has minimal impact on performance. Do not test multiple primary texts or headlines. Write one strong version and use it across every creative in the campaign. The only variable you test is the creative itself. Testing copy variables wastes budget and corrupts data.

**Rule 6: Never pause the account.**
Every time an account pauses for any reason, Meta's learning phase resets. It takes 7–14 days of uninterrupted spend to re-optimize. Keep the card current. Do not pause campaigns to "test." Never pause a winning ad.

**Rule 7: UGC video first, statics second.**
Video outperforms static for B2C and local services. Run mostly UGC with one or two statics alongside. The best performing static the agency ever ran was a Notes app screenshot that took 3 minutes to make. Lo-fi stops the scroll better than polished graphics.

**Rule 8: Find a winner and repeat it. Do not chase novelty.**
The agency has run the same ad since November 2024 and it is still their best performer. Once you find a winning hook and creative, make more of the same. 70% of effort = repeat proven winners. 20% = variations on winners. 10% = brand new experiments. Most people never find a winner because they keep changing things before anything can optimize.

## PART 5: ALEX HORMOZI — $100M HOOKS PLAYBOOK

### The Most Important Advertising Principle
"After you've written your headline, you've spent eighty cents of your advertising dollar." — David Ogilvy

If you have ten hours to advertise, spend eight of them on the hook. The hook sells the person on paying attention. Nothing else matters if the hook fails.

### What a Hook Is
A hook has two parts:
1. **Call-out** — gets the prospect to say "this is for me." Works like hearing your own name in a noisy room (the cocktail party effect).
2. **Promise of value** — if I keep watching/reading, I get something. This is the condition: if you do this thing, you will get this value.

### Types of Verbal Hooks (with examples from Hormozi's top performers)

**Labels (Call-Outs):** Name your avatar directly.
- "Local business owners, I have a gift for you."
- "If you run an HVAC company..."
- "Homeowners in Cary and Apex..."

**Statements (47.9% of winning hooks — #1 type):** Bold, specific, declarative.
- "The smartest thing you can do today..."
- "How to get ahead of 99% of people."
- "Your driveway is making your house look older than it is."

**Questions:** Two types.
- Yes questions: "Would you pay $1,000 to have the business of your dreams in 30 days?"
- Open questions: "Which would you rather be?"

**Conditionals:** "If you [condition], then [result]." Self-selects audience and promises value.
- "If you're working all the time and your business isn't growing, you're working on the wrong stuff."
- "If you own a home in Cary and Apex, here's exactly what we'll do for you."

**Commands:** Direct instruction paired with a benefit.
- "Read this if you're tired of being broke."
- "Watch this if you want to fill your empty beds."

**Narratives/Stories:** Story-led openers (only 3% of winners — use sparingly).
- "One day I was on a job and a homeowner came out and said..."

**Exclamations:** Strong emotion or surprise.
- "Ahhhh... this is the blueprint..."

### Nonverbal Hooks
Hooks are not just words. Visual and audio elements also hook:
- A loud sound, an unexpected visual, a striking before/after image
- Text on screen that appears suddenly
- Someone doing something surprising in the first frame
- Memes or meme-like content (attract widest audiences)

### Distribution of Hormozi's 121 Best Hooks
| Type | % of Winners |
|------|-------------|
| Statements | 47.9% |
| Commands | 21.5% |
| Questions | 9.9% |
| Exclamations | 9.1% |
| Lists/Steps | 5.8% |
| Stories | 3% |
| Conditionals | 2.5% |

Write statements first. Almost half of all winning hooks are simple declarative sentences.

### The 70/20/10 Hook Rule (from Google's Innovation Model)
- **70%:** Proven winners you already know work. Reuse and remix your best hooks. This is the bread-and-butter. Stabilizes performance and guarantees baseline results.
- **20%:** Winner-adjacent. Same formula applied to a new angle. Promising but less proven. Example: if "If you run an HVAC company" works, try "If you run a plumbing company."
- **10%:** Brand new experiments. Risky, potentially huge wins. If an experiment wins, it moves to the 70% bucket. If it loses, document and do not repeat.

Most businesses do 100% new every time and wonder why nothing sticks.

### Where to Find Hooks
1. Your own past winning ads — literally reuse them
2. Your own best-performing organic content (clips, tweets, emails)
3. Other people's ads saved to your phone (swipe file)
4. Other people's high-view organic content — steal the hook format
5. Platform ad libraries — use sparingly, hard to tell what actually performs

### Testing Hooks
For ads: record 10 hook variations for every 1 piece of ad content. 10x hooks per piece of content. This is how you scale without burning out on new ideas.

## PART 6: ALEX HORMOZI — GOATed ADS PLAYBOOK

### The Core Problem This Solves
Most advertisers hit a wall at a certain spend level. They blame "market saturation." The real problem is ad quality, not market size. Better ads convert a bigger percentage of a larger audience. The only way to make better ads consistently is to make more ads, through a production system.

### The Ad Assembly Formula
Instead of thinking of ads as one complete piece, break them into three parts and assemble combinations:

**50 Hooks × 3-5 Meats × 1-3 CTAs = 150-750 Ads Per Week**

This is how Hormozi scaled Acquisition.com and Skool.com. The company that inspired this doubled in two quarters after switching to this system.

### Time Allocation for Recording Sessions
- **80% of prep time on hooks.** If they don't make it through the hook, nothing else matters.
- **20% on the meat (body).** The body burns out slower because fewer people see it — they only see it after passing the hook. 3-5 meats per weekly recording session is enough.
- **~0% on CTAs.** Find one or two that work and keep using them. Do not overthink CTAs.

Six-pack abs are made in the kitchen, not in the gym. Ads are made in research, not in recording. 90% of the work is preparation, not production.

### The 5 Types of Meat (Ad Body)
These are the only formats Hormozi uses. They work across every industry: services, education, physical products, brick-and-mortar, software.

**1. Demonstration Ads**
Think: live use, before/after, unboxing, product in use, comparisons, high production hero ads (Dollar Shave Club style).
Best for: Any service with a visible transformation. Pressure washing before/after, cleaned HVAC unit, clean driveway vs dirty driveway.

**2. Testimonial Ads**
Think: UGC, raw iPhone, direct to camera, podcast style, walk-and-talk, group testimonials ("parade of proof"), lifecycle ads, man-on-the-street interviews.
Best for: When you have real client results to show. Record them candidly, not staged. Raw iPhone outperforms studio.

**3. Education Ads**
Think: explainers, how-to, whiteboard, "here's what most people don't know."
Best for: B2B where the mechanism needs explaining. Subutai AI would use this to explain how the voice AI works.

**4. Story Ads**
Think: problem-solution narrative, lifestyle transformation, founder journey.
Best for: Creating emotional connection. Use when proof is limited.

**5. Faceless Ads**
Think: screenshots, text-only, slideshows, Notes app screenshots, screen recordings.
Best for: Notes app static ads. Often the highest-performing static format. Agency's best-ever static ad was a Notes app screenshot made in 3 minutes.

### The CTA Formula (Show AND Tell)
A great CTA covers all five of these:
1. What to do: "Tap the link below"
2. How to do it: "Fill out the 60-second form"
3. When to do it: "Right now" / "Today only"
4. What they get: "Your free quote" / "A confirmed spot on our calendar"
5. What happens next: "We'll call you back same day"

Example: "Tap the link below [what + how], right now [when], to get your free quote in 60 seconds [what they get], and we'll call you back same day [what happens next]."

If possible, physically demonstrate clicking the button or filling out the form on camera.

### Scaling Past the Wall
When you hit a performance ceiling:
1. First: refresh creative (new hooks, same winning format)
2. Second: expand awareness level of hooks (go colder — see Awareness Levels section)
3. Third: try new meat formats
The ceiling is almost never the market. It is almost always ad quality and creative volume.

## PART 7: AWARENESS LEVELS (Eugene Schwartz via Hormozi)

The audience's awareness level determines what type of hook and body to use. Ads start profitably at the warmest audience (Most Aware) and become less profitable as they reach colder traffic. Better creative lets you profitably convert colder audiences.

| Level | State | Hook Style | Body Style | Example Hook |
|-------|-------|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| 1. Most Aware | Knows you, just needs the deal | Offer-driven | Just make the offer | "50% off this month only." |
| 2. Product-Aware | Knows what you sell, unsure about you | Proof-driven | Show results and credibility | "Why 500 homeowners chose Blue Wave last year." |
| 3. Solution-Aware | Knows solutions exist, doesn't know yours | Promise-driven | Introduce your mechanism | "The fastest way to get your home pressure washed right." |
| 4. Problem-Aware | Knows they have a problem, no solution yet | Pain-driven | Agitate problem, then present solution | "Your driveway is making your house look older than it is." |
| 5. Unaware | Doesn't know they have a problem | Curiosity-driven | Create awareness of hidden problem | "The hidden thing that's draining your home value." |

**Where Meta cold traffic sits for each client type:**
- **Local home services (Blue Wave):** Level 4 (Problem-Aware). They know their driveway is dirty. Hook should name the problem or the result, not the product.
- **B2B services (Subutai AI, Scalevertise):** Level 3 (Solution-Aware). They know they're losing leads or missing calls. They just don't know your specific solution exists.

**Expansion strategy:** If you're only writing hooks for Level 1-2, you're only talking to your warmest audience. To scale, write hooks that work at Level 3, 4, and 5. Each level requires a different hook type but the same body can often follow.

## PART 8: UGC SCRIPT STRUCTURE

A UGC video is NOT three sentences. It is 60–90 seconds of structured talking on camera. The person filming does not need to be the founder. Tone is casual and direct — like telling a neighbor, not performing for a camera.

### The Master UGC Script Template

**Beat 1: Niche Call-Out (first 3 seconds — this is the hook)**
Name the exact audience. Be hyper-specific. The wrong person should scroll past immediately. The right person stops. Include location for local services. Include job/role qualifier for B2B.
- B2C: "If you own a home in Cary, Apex, Raleigh, or Pittsboro..."
- B2B: "If you run an HVAC company and you're missing calls while your guys are on jobs..."

**Beat 2: Result + Offer (immediately after the call-out)**
Tell them the exact thing you will do for them and the offer. Do NOT make them wait for this. It comes right after the call-out, not at the end of the video.
- "...here's exactly what we'll do. We will come out one time, wash your house and driveway, and have both looking brand new by the end of the day. And right now if you book both together you save $150."

**Beat 3: How It Works (mechanism, 2–3 sentences max)**
Explain the process simply. What happens, in what order, how long it takes. Answers the "but how does it actually work?" question in their head.

**Beat 4: Proof / Credibility (a real number or result)**
A specific number. A client result. A before/after shown on camera. Something that makes the claim believable. "We've done this for X homes" or "here's what last week looked like" with a visual.

**Beat 5: Guarantee (risk removal)**
Remove the last objection. Must be specific. "If you're not happy, we come back and redo it free." "10 jobs in 30 days or you don't pay." Generic guarantees do not work.

**Beat 6: CTA (one action, spelled out)**
One thing to do. Tell them exactly what they get when they do it and how fast it happens. "Tap the link below. Free quote in 60 seconds. I'll get back to you same day."

### Filming Rules
- Phone camera is fine — no studio needed
- Film on location at a job site or in context
- Casual tone like telling a neighbor
- Hold up phone to show before/after during proof section
- No need to memorize word for word — know the beats, be natural
- Film multiple hook takes separately — the hook is the most important part

### Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Leading with the company name instead of a call-out
- Burying the offer at the end of the video
- No proof or credibility beat included
- Too short (under 45 seconds is not long enough to sell)
- Sounding like a polished ad instead of a person talking
- Founder-focused language ("I built this company...") instead of customer-focused language
- Using em dashes (—) anywhere in the script or copy

## PART 9: THE OFFER FORMULA (Hormozi Value Equation)

### The Core Principle
Sell the result, not the tool. Sell the outcome, not the service. Nobody wants an AI receptionist. They want booked jobs. Nobody wants a pressure wash. They want a home that looks new.

### Hormozi's Value Equation
**Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Success) / (Time Delay × Effort and Sacrifice)**

A great offer:
- Maximizes the dream outcome (the result they actually want, not what you sell)
- Maximizes perceived likelihood it will work (guarantee, proof, specificity)
- Minimizes time delay (fast results, specific timeline)
- Minimizes effort (done for you, no work required from them)

Hit all four components or the offer is weak.

### Weak Offer vs Strong Offer

| Element | Weak Version | Strong Version |
|---------|-------------|----------------|
| Dream Outcome | "An AI receptionist for your business" | "10 extra booked HVAC jobs this month" |
| Likelihood | "Results may vary" | "10 jobs in 30 days or you don't pay until it does" |
| Time Delay | "See results over time" | "Live in 48 hours. Results in 30 days." |
| Effort | "Set it up yourself" | "Done for you. You just show up to the jobs." |

### Rules for Building an Offer
1. Include a specific number. "More leads" is not an offer. "20–30 qualified appointments" is an offer.
2. Include a timeframe. "In 30 days" is concrete. "Over time" is not.
3. Include a guarantee that removes risk. "Or you don't pay" is the gold standard.
4. For local services where pricing varies, use a discount bundle. "$150 off when you book both" is concrete even when total varies.
5. Name the mechanism. "Our Resident Acquisition System" sounds more proprietary than "our service." Naming the mechanism makes it feel unique even if the underlying method is similar to competitors.

### Offer Examples by Client
- **Scalevertise (care homes):** "20–30 qualified resident appointments in 30 days or your management fee is waived. Done for you. One home per area."
- **Subutai AI (HVAC):** "We install an AI receptionist that books you 10 extra jobs from missed calls in your first 30 days, or you don't pay until it does. First 50 calls free."
- **Blue Wave Pro Wash:** "We come out one time, wash your house and driveway, and have both looking brand new same day. Book the bundle and save $150. Limited spots per month."

## PART 10: STATIC AD FORMATS

Run statics alongside UGC. Never instead of UGC.

### Format 1: Notes App Screenshot (Top Performer)
A screenshot of a plain text note from your phone. Black text on white (or white on black). One or two emojis max. No design, no branding, no graphic.

**Why it works:** Looks organic. Stops the scroll because it does not look like an ad. The agency's best-performing ad of all time is this format and took 3 minutes to make.

**Formula:**
- EMOJI + AUDIENCE CALL-OUT IN CAPS
- One sentence: what you'll do for them + the offer
- One sentence: urgency (limited spots, this month only)

## PART 11: LEAD FOLLOW-UP SYSTEM

### The Core Problem
Ads generate leads. Follow-up speed decides whether those leads become jobs. This is the most ignored part of every funnel and the most expensive mistake clients make.

### The Data (from Hormozi's ALAN lead automation system research)
- **21x** more likely to close a lead contacted within 5 minutes vs 30 minutes
- **80%** drop in conversion rate when follow-up happens after 30 minutes
- **60 seconds** is approximately how long a motivated lead stays hot before scrolling to the next ad

**What actually happens to a slow lead:** They fill out the form. They keep scrolling Facebook. A competitor's ad shows up. That company texts them in 30 seconds. By the time the client calls an hour later, the homeowner doesn't remember filling out the form and has already booked with someone else. The lead was not bad. It went cold.

**What Hormozi's research found (from ALAN data on 4,000+ appointments/day):**
Four factors that significantly increase show rates:
1. More available time slots = higher show rate
2. Fewer days out the appointment is scheduled = higher show rate
3. More follow-up attempts = higher show rate
4. When leads respond to follow-up = higher show rate

### The Fix: Instant Automated SMS
Set up through Zapier connecting Facebook Lead Ads to GoHighLevel (or any SMS tool). Takes about 20 minutes to configure. Fires the second someone submits the lead form.

**Template:**
"Hey [First Name]! This is [Owner Name] from [Business]. Just saw your request come through and I'm pulling together your quote now. I'll give you a call shortly. Feel free to reply here with any questions."

### Rule for Every Client
Instant automated follow-up is non-negotiable before scaling spend. Running ads without it is filling a bucket with a hole in it. The ads can be perfect and the client will still say the leads are bad quality. They are not bad. They went cold. Fix the follow-up first.

## PART 12: HORMOZI — ADDITIONAL FRAMEWORKS

### From the Pricing Playbook
**Three ways to grow revenue (in order of effort):**
1. Get more customers (hardest — requires ad spend, acquisition cost)
2. Get existing customers to buy more often (easier — requires offers and follow-up)
3. Raise prices (easiest — requires confidence and better framing)

Most agencies immediately go to #1. Hormozi says double your prices first.

**Key pricing rules:**
- Charge what the result is worth, not what the service costs you to deliver
- A guaranteed outcome justifies a premium price. Risk reversal (the guarantee) makes higher prices easier to charge, not harder.
- Raising prices filters out bad clients and attracts better ones
- Annual billing = more cash upfront, lower churn

**For Scalevertise specifically:** $2,000/month flat fee is defensible when the offer is 20–30 qualified appointments. The client spends far more on empty beds per month than our fee. Frame it as: one filled bed per month covers the entire management fee.

### From the Closing Playbook
**Hormozi's philosophy on objections:** Every objection is a form of "blame" — the prospect is blaming something (time, money, spouse, past experiences) for why they can't buy. Your job is not to argue with the blame. Your job is to remove it.

**Rules for closing:**
1. Never chase. Following up aggressively signals low value. Set a clear next step on every call.
2. Objections are not rejections. "I need to think about it" means "I don't understand the value yet."
3. The guarantee does more closing than any pitch. If someone is still unsure after a strong guarantee, they have an undisclosed objection.
4. Ask "What would need to be true for this to be a no-brainer for you?" — then solve that specific thing.

**Common objections and responses for Scalevertise:**
- "I can't afford it" → "You're currently losing [X × empty beds × $3,000/month] per month. The management fee is $2,000. This pays for itself if we fill one bed."
- "I need to think about it" → "What's the one thing you're unsure about? Let me answer that now."
- "I've tried ads before and they didn't work" → "What agency ran them? Were they specific to care homes? Most agencies run generic ads. This is built specifically for this industry with a guarantee."

### From the Lead Nurture Playbook (Hormozi's ALAN Research)
**Four pillars of lead nurture (from machine learning analysis of 4,000+ daily appointments):**

1. **Availability:** More available time slots = more people book and show. Don't limit your calendar to 2 slots per week. Open it up.
2. **Speed:** The faster you respond to a new lead, the more likely they are to show up. Under 5 minutes is the target. Automated SMS achieves this.
3. **Personalization:** Leads who feel like a human engaged with them show up more. Personalized first name + reference to what they submitted > generic "someone will call you."
4. **Volume:** More follow-up attempts = higher show rate. Most people stop at 1–2 attempts. The data shows you need significantly more touchpoints. Sequence: immediate SMS → call same day → SMS next morning → call day 3 → email day 5 → SMS day 7.

### From the Branding Playbook
**What brand actually is:** Brand is what people say about you when you're not in the room. It is reputation, not a logo. Brand is built through consistent delivery of the promise, not through design.

**For Scalevertise:** Brand = we are the only Meta ads agency that specializes exclusively in care homes and guarantees results or waives our fee. Every touchpoint should reinforce this specificity. The exclusivity angle (one home per area) strengthens brand by creating scarcity and perceived prestige.

### From the Marketing Machine Playbook
**The goal of every ad:** Get the right person to take one specific action. Not to educate. Not to build brand awareness. Not to tell the full story. One action. Usually: fill out the form, book a call, or click to learn more. Design the entire ad around making that one action as easy and obvious as possible.

**Lifecycle advertising:** As your audience grows, segment your ads:
- Cold traffic (never heard of you) → Awareness hooks, problem/solution format
- Warm traffic (visited your site, engaged with content) → Retargeting with proof and offer
- Hot traffic (submitted a lead form, on your list) → Direct offer, urgency, testimonials

## PART 13: COPY RULES FOR ALL OUTPUT

These apply to every ad, script, email, strategy doc, or piece of copy produced by this system:

1. **No em dashes (—) anywhere.** Use commas, periods, or rephrase. This is a hard rule enforced by the agency.
2. **No founder-centered language** unless specifically asked. The person filming is the business, not "the founder."
3. **No corporate speak.** No "leverage," "synergy," "utilize," "optimize." Write like a person talks.
4. **No vague offers.** "More leads" is not an offer. "20–30 qualified appointments in 30 days" is an offer.
5. **Specificity over generality.** Specific numbers, timeframes, locations, and guarantees always outperform vague claims.
6. **One action per CTA.** Never give two options. "Click here or call us" is two options. "Click below for a free quote" is one.
7. **The call-out is always first.** Never open a script, ad, or hook without naming the audience. The creative does the targeting.
8. **No generic social proof.** "Hundreds of satisfied customers" is useless. "We've washed 300+ homes in Cary and Apex" is real.
9. **Guarantees must be specific and bold.** "Satisfaction guaranteed" is worthless. "20–30 appointments in 30 days or you don't pay our management fee" is a guarantee.
10. **Test hooks, not copy.** When running multiple ads, keep primary text and headline the same. Change only the creative (video/image) and hook.
