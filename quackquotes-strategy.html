Section 02
Revenue Split Analysis

An honest breakdown of whether QuackQuotes' current pricing tiers work for contractors and the business.

Under $200
35%
Verdict: Too high for this tier. On a $150 pest control treatment, QuackQuotes takes $52.50 and the contractor keeps $97.50. For a job that costs them gas, time, and supplies, that margin will feel painful. This is the tier most likely to cause contractor churn or complaints. Compare to Thumbtack charging $15-75 per lead upfront regardless of win. The no-upfront-cost advantage softens it, but 35% is still aggressive on small jobs.
Recommendation: Drop to 28-30% for sub-$200 jobs.
$200-$600
25%
Verdict: Solid. This is the sweet spot. On a $400 pressure washing job, QuackQuotes takes $100 and the contractor pockets $300. That feels fair when they paid nothing upfront, faced no lead cost, and just showed up with a quote. This tier covers the majority of pest control, gutter cleaning, junk removal, and pressure washing jobs. Most contractors will accept this without pushback.
Recommendation: Keep at 25%. This tier is well-positioned.
Over $600
15%
Verdict: Good, but consider adding a cap. On a $1,000 electrical job, 15% is $150 to QuackQuotes. That is reasonable. But if this tier reaches into roofing or HVAC replacement territory ($6,000-12,000 jobs), 15% becomes $900-1,800 per job which will face serious contractor resistance. Adding a maximum dollar cap ($300 max commission on any single job) would remove that friction while still protecting high-ticket revenue.
Recommendation: Add a $300 commission cap on any single job for jobs above $2,000.
Overall Verdict
The model is fundamentally strong. Paying only on a win is a genuinely better deal than Angi, HomeAdvisor, or Thumbtack for contractors. The percentage structure just needs small adjustments: lower the sub-$200 tier slightly and add a high-ticket cap. With those tweaks this becomes one of the most contractor-friendly pricing models in the home services space.

Section 03
Grand Slam Offers

Built on Hormozi's value equation: Dream Outcome x Likelihood of Achievement, divided by Time Delay x Effort. The goal is to make saying no feel stupid.

Offer 01 — Primary
Get Paid First. Pay Us Second.
9.5/10
The full pitch: "Join QuackQuotes for free. We run ads, collect job requests from homeowners in your area, and put your profile, your price, and your reviews in front of them. You only pay us a percentage of the job after the customer pays you. If you don't win, you pay nothing. Not a cent. Not ever."

Why this is a Grand Slam: Every objection a contractor has is dead on arrival. "What if it doesn't work?" You pay nothing. "What if I can't afford it?" There is nothing to afford. "What if the leads are bad?" You don't pay for bad leads. The risk is completely reversed. The only thing a contractor has to lose by joining is time spent writing a quote. That is it. Hormozi calls this making the "offer so good people feel stupid saying no." This is that offer.
Apply to: All niches. All ads. This is the lead offer in every hook, every body script, and every static ad. Never bury this. Lead with it.
Offer 02 — Launch Incentive
Your First 3 Wins Are Commission-Free
9/10
The full pitch: "The first three jobs you win through QuackQuotes, we take zero commission. You keep every dollar. After that, the normal percentage kicks in only when you win and get paid."

Why this works: It collapses the time between joining and trusting. Most contractors will not believe the platform works until they see it work. The first three wins are proof. Once a contractor has won three jobs through you with zero commission taken, they are already trained to use the platform. By the time commission starts they have momentum and they already trust you. Use this as a limited-time launch offer for the first wave of contractors in each niche. Scarcity makes it urgent. "We are only doing this for the first 20 pest control operators who join in Raleigh."
Apply to: Launch campaigns. First 30 days in each new niche. Mention in the body script, not the hook. The hook leads with Offer 01.

Guarantees

Hormozi's rule: the guarantee should make it feel irrational to say no. The stronger the guarantee, the higher the price you can charge and the faster people move. Graded on boldness, believability, and risk to QuackQuotes.

Guarantee 01 — Use This One
"If You Don't Win, You Don't Pay. Ever."
10/10
This is not a guarantee. It is the business model. And that is exactly what makes it so powerful. The guarantee and the offer are the same thing, which means it is 100% believable and 100% true. No fine print. No conditions. If a contractor does not win a job, they owe QuackQuotes literally zero dollars. Say this in every ad, on every landing page, and in every sales conversation. Say it more than once. Most people do not believe it the first time.
Risk to QuackQuotes: Zero. This costs nothing to promise because it is already how the platform works.
Guarantee 02
"5 Real Job Opportunities in 30 Days or We Work for Free"
8.5/10
We guarantee your profile will receive at least 5 legitimate quote requests from homeowners in your niche within 30 days of joining. If we fall short, your commission rate drops to zero for the following 30 days on every job you win. This is a strong guarantee because it puts delivery on us, not the contractor. It also forces QuackQuotes to actually run ads aggressively in that niche, which is a good internal forcing function.
Risk to QuackQuotes: Low-Medium. Only activate this guarantee once ads are consistently producing in a niche. Do not promise this on day one.
Guarantee 03
"More Opportunities Than Angi in 60 Days or Your Next 5 Wins Are Free"
7.5/10
We will show your profile to more homeowners than Angi would have sent you leads for the same 60-day period. If we don't, your next 5 won jobs are commission-free. This is a direct comparison guarantee and it is bold. The math almost always favors QuackQuotes because Angi charges per lead regardless of outcome so contractors get fewer leads per dollar spent. This guarantee works especially well on contractors who are currently paying Angi and frustrated with the results.
Risk to QuackQuotes: Medium. Requires tracking and honesty. Only use with contractors who can show you their Angi spend so you can verify.
Guarantee 04
"Win Your First Job in 14 Days or We Set Up Your Google Ads For Free"
7/10
If you don't win at least one job through QuackQuotes within 14 days of your profile going live, we will personally set up a Google Business Profile optimization for your company at no cost. This type of guarantee, called a "service guarantee" by Hormozi, adds a bonus that has real perceived value even though the cost to deliver it is low. Google profile optimization takes 2 hours and helps the contractor regardless of whether they stay on the platform.
Risk to QuackQuotes: Low. The cost to deliver is time only. Great to use during launch when ad volume is still building and you cannot yet reliably promise job volume.
How to Stack These
In every ad: lead with Guarantee 01. It is your hook, your headline, and your close all in one. In the body script: reinforce it by saying it a second time in different words. On the landing page: show it in bold above the form. In sales conversations: use Guarantee 02 or 03 depending on how skeptical the contractor is. The goal is to make them feel like they would be leaving money on the table by not joining.
Section 05
Targeting Strategy

Broad targeting with a general landing page vs. niche-specific ads. Here is the honest answer.

Option A
Broad "Home Service Businesses" Ad
  • Larger addressable audience in Meta
  • One ad to run instead of many
  • Landing page asks what niche they are in
  • Lower creative production cost
The problem: A pest control owner scrolling Facebook stops when they see "pest control." They do not stop for "home service businesses." Generic ads get skipped. The landing page cannot fix a weak hook.
Option B (Recommended)
Niche-Specific Ads per Service Type
  • Hook speaks directly to the person watching
  • Far higher stop-rate and click-through
  • Lower CPL because relevance is high
  • Each ad goes to a niche-specific or general landing page
  • Easy to test which niche converts cheapest
Start with pest control ads. Once that is converting, add gutter cleaning, then junk removal. Scale what works.
Landing Page Recommendation

One universal landing page is fine as long as the headline matches the niche in the ad. Use URL parameters or separate landing pages per niche. The form can ask "What type of home service do you provide?" as the first field so you capture the niche data without needing 10 separate pages. The critical thing is that the first thing they see on the landing page matches exactly what the ad told them they would find.
