Section 01

Why The Current Ads Are Underperforming

We had our ad strategy reviewed by a specialist agency that spends $40k+/month on Meta for AI and service businesses. The verdict was consistent and it comes down to one core idea:

The one rule everything hangs on
"The creative is everything. Broad targeting, max audience. The creative does the targeting, not the audience settings. If the right person doesn't feel called out in the first second, the ad fails, no matter who you target."

Measured against that standard, here is an honest grade of where we are today.

AreaGradeWhat's wrong right now
Niche call-out Needs work Ads don't open by naming who they're for. A prospect can't tell in one second if it's for them. There's no "If you run an HVAC business, this is for you."
Targeting one audience Needs work We've been spreading across audiences/niches. Recommendation: one niche, multiple creatives, broad targeting (age 21-50, max audience), let Meta find them.
Campaign structure Fixable We were splitting budget evenly and cutting ads early. The pro move is CBO, one ad set, and let Meta push spend to the winner. One ad eating the budget is the system working, not a problem.
Copy formula Fixable Current copy explains the product. It should follow: niche call-out → result they want → the mechanism → the guarantee → CTA.
Testing approach Fixable We were testing many headlines/primary texts. Primary text has minimal impact. Use ONE primary text + ONE headline across all ads, and only test the creative.
Creative format Fixable Need mostly UGC video (best quality) plus a couple of simple "notes app" static ads. Their single best performer is a 3-minute notes-app screenshot.
The offer Needs work Current offer sells the tool ("$299/mo, free trial"). It should sell a specific result to a specific person with a guarantee. Options on the next page.
Landing page Strong, but The page looks great. For paid traffic the advice is to strip it down and optimize purely for the booking. "Pretty" can actually lower conversions. Options later in this doc.
Section 02

Decision 1 — Which Niche Do We Target?

The single biggest lever. The agency was blunt: "service based business" is too broad, even "small business" is too broad. We pick ONE niche so the ads, the landing page, and the language all speak to one person. The company can still serve everyone. This is only the niche we point paid ads at.

Note: we are NOT recommending the company drop other industries. This is about giving Meta one clear target so the ads actually convert. We can always open a second niche later with its own campaign.

Option A · Recommended

HVAC & Home Services

The missed-call pain is the most extreme here. Techs are under a house or on a roof, the phone rings, and the job goes to whoever picked up. Jobs are high-ticket ($5k–$14k), so one recovered call pays for the service many times over. The page already leads with "$14,000 recovered in missed installs in 11 days."

+ Strongest emotional hook, highest job value, proof already exists.

– Proof case studies on the page are currently dental/restaurant, not HVAC.

Option B

Dental & Orthodontics

Our strongest proof lives here (Image Orthodontics, $401,500 in one quarter). After-hours new-patient calls are a clear, expensive leak. Easy to make the numbers believable because the case study is real.

+ Best existing proof and testimonials.

– Practices often already have front-desk staff, so the "missed call" pain feels slightly softer than HVAC.

Option C

Restaurants & Hospitality

Menlo Tavern case study ($117,000) supports this. Reservations missed during peak hours are an obvious problem.

+ Real proof, very relatable problem.

– Lower ticket value per booking, thinner margins, harder to justify $299/mo emotionally.

Option D

Legal / Professional Services

High case value, every missed call could be a major client. Law firms feel a missed lead acutely.

+ Very high client value, serious buyers.

– More compliance-sensitive, longer sales cycle, they may resist an AI answering.

Our recommendation

  1. Run HVAC first. Best pain, best dollar story, easiest to make people stop scrolling.
  2. Keep dental as the obvious second niche once HVAC is profitable (it has the strongest proof).
  3. Whatever we pick, the ads + the landing page should match that one niche.
Section 03

Decision 2 — The Offer

Right now the offer sells the tool: "$299/mo, free trial, first 50 calls free." The agency's point: sell the result to a specific person, with a guarantee that removes risk. The $299/mo stays exactly as it is on the back end. The offer is just the wrapper that gets the call booked. Here are three ways to frame it (written for HVAC, easily swapped for any niche).

Option A · Recommended

The Booked Jobs Guarantee

"We install a 24/7 AI receptionist that answers every call in under 2 seconds and books the job straight into your calendar. In your first 30 days it books you more in jobs than it costs you, or you don't pay. First 50 calls free, no card."

+ Sells the outcome (jobs), removes risk, time-bound, easy to believe.

Option B

The Missed-Call Recovery Offer

"Most HVAC owners lose 10–20 jobs a month to voicemail and never know it. We plug that leak with an AI receptionist that answers in 2 seconds, 24/7. Free for your first 50 calls. If it doesn't pay for itself in recovered jobs, you walk away."

+ Leads with the hidden problem (lost money), low-friction free trial.

– Slightly softer than a hard numeric guarantee.

Option C

The ROI / Dollar-Figure Offer

"Your AI receptionist will recover at least $10,000 in missed-call jobs in your first 90 days, or we work for free until it does." (Mirrors the proven "10 clients in 90 days or free" structure from top agencies.)

+ A dollar figure is the most concrete promise of all.

– Harder to attribute cleanly; needs solid call tracking to defend.

Why any of these beats the current offer

Hormozi's value equation: a great offer maximizes the dream outcome and the likelihood of success, while minimizing time and effort. "10 extra booked jobs in 30 days, done for you, live in 48 hours, or you don't pay" hits all four. "$299/mo for an AI receptionist" hits none of them.

Section 04

Decision 3 — Campaign Structure & Testing

This is the part we were doing wrong mechanically. The fixes are simple and they're how the top agencies run every account.

Stop doing
  • Splitting budget evenly across ads
  • Cutting ads early before Meta optimizes
  • Testing many headlines + primary texts at once
  • Narrow / detailed interest targeting
  • Multiple niches in one account on low spend
Start doing
  • CBO, one ad set
  • Broad targeting: age 21-50, max audience, no interests
  • Let Meta push spend to the winner
  • One primary text + one headline on every ad
  • Test ONLY the creative
  • One niche, multiple creatives
The mindset shift on CBO

When one ad eats most of the budget, that's not a bug. That's Meta telling you which creative wins. Trust the platform. It's a better tester than any of us. Find a winner, then run it for months. Their best ad has been running, unchanged, since 2024.

The shared copy (write once, use on all 5 ads):

Primary text — same on every ad

If you run an HVAC business, every call you miss is a job your competitor just booked.

Our AI receptionist answers every call in under 2 seconds, qualifies the job, and books it straight into your calendar. 24/7. Even at 2am on a holiday weekend.

In your first 30 days it books you more in jobs than it costs you, or you don't pay.

First 50 calls are free. Tap below to see if your area is open.

Headline: Stop Losing HVAC Jobs To Voicemail

Section 06

Decision 4 — The Landing Page

The current page (the dedicated lead-capture funnel) is clean and on-brand. The agency's only note: for paid traffic, strip it down and optimize purely for the booking. "Looking pretty can actually be a bad thing" if it adds distractions between the click and the booking. Three ways to handle it:

Option A · Recommended

Build one niche-specific stripped funnel

A dedicated HVAC page: HVAC headline, the $14k proof, the guarantee, the lead form, one CTA. No nav, no extra sections. Ads point here. Keep the full site live for organic/direct traffic.

+ Highest conversion, message-matches the ad exactly.

Option B

Trim the existing page

Keep the current page but remove the top nav and any links that let people wander off, and move the form higher.

+ Less work, keeps the design we like.

– Still not fully optimized; some leakage remains.

Option C

VSL-style page

Single page: headline, a short founder/UGC video, one CTA to book. Mirrors what high-spend agencies use.

+ Proven structure for cold paid traffic.

– Needs a strong video produced first.

What we need from you to move

  1. Niche: approve HVAC, or pick another (Section 02).
  2. Offer: approve the Booked Jobs Guarantee, or pick B/C (Section 03).
  3. Creatives: approve the 5 scripts. We'll need you (or talent) to film the 3 UGC videos.
  4. Landing page: green-light the stripped HVAC funnel.

Nothing here is final. This is a menu, not a mandate. Tell us what fits your vision for Subutai and we'll build exactly that.

Section 07

Terms of Service — 30-Day Performance Guarantee

Legal note

Have your lawyer review this before it goes live. This is commercial language, not legal advice.

In your first 30 days after your AI receptionist goes live, it books you more in jobs than your subscription costs for that period, or that month is free.

How it works

  • The 30-day window starts the day your AI receptionist goes live and is fully connected to your call forwarding and calendar, not the day you sign up.
  • A "booked job" is any inbound call your AI receptionist answers and schedules as an appointment, in your service area, for a real service request from a prospective or existing customer.
  • "Books you more than it costs you" means the combined value of the jobs it books in those 30 days is greater than the subscription fee for that period. In practice, a single recovered HVAC job almost always clears this on its own.

What we ask of you (the guarantee depends on this)

  • All inbound calls you want covered are forwarded to your AI receptionist line for the full 30 days.
  • The service stays connected and is not paused or disconnected during the window.
  • You respond to and honor the appointments the AI books for you.
  • You complete onboarding and give us the access we need to set it up correctly.

If we fall short

If the conditions above were met and your AI receptionist did not book you more in jobs than it cost you in the first 30 days, that month is free. Choose one and put it in writing before the client signs: we do not charge you for that period, or we refund the fee you paid for that period.

30-Day Money-Back

On top of the performance guarantee, if you are not satisfied within your first 30 days and you met the conditions above, you can request a full refund of the fees you paid for that period.

First 50 calls

Your first 50 calls are free with no card and no setup fee, so there is no cost to you before the service has had a real chance to work.
